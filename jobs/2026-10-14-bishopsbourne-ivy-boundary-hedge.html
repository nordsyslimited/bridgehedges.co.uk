Long Ivy-Covered Boundary Hedge, Near Bishopsbourne

A boundary hedge is easy to ignore until it is two metres thick. This one, running down the side of a long lawn near Bishopsbourne, had been left for several seasons, and the ivy had taken the opportunity to climb all the way up.

What was there

Mixed woody growth, with a thick coat of ivy on the face and a mass of bare, whippy stems showing where the hedge had been left to reach for the light. At the base there was a build-up of leaf litter and dead stems, and the hedge had spread across a good strip of the lawn. Further along, an old timber retaining edge was almost buried.

The considered clip

Our approach to a hedge like this is to look first and cut second. The aim was not to scalp it, but to bring the face back to a line that is sensible for the hedge and sustainable for the owner. We took the width back in stages, thinned the ivy so it no longer smothered the host plants, and tidied the base so the retaining edge showed again.

The cuttings were raked from the lawn and taken away, and the grass was left clean so the owner could mow the edge straight away.

An honest quote

On a job like this it is tempting to upsell. We do not. You get a clear quote for the work that the hedge needs, and nothing added. If you are not happy with the job, you do not pay.
