Leaning evergreen hedge brought back to a clean line, Patrixbourne

The problem

In Patrixbourne an evergreen hedge beside a building had slowly lost its edges. One side had begun to lean out over the grass, and the top had gone wavy where it had been trimmed at different heights in different years.

It was not a dramatic overgrowth, just a hedge that had stopped looking considered.

What we did

We set the line first, using the building as a guide, and cut both faces back to it so the hedge stood upright again. The top was levelled along its whole length in one pass so there were no steps where sections met.

We do not upsell on a job like this. The hedge needed a proper cut and a tidy, and that is what it got. Growth was left on the green side of the wood so it recovers quickly. Everything was bagged and the lawn was left clear.

The result

A clean line, an even top and a lawn that can be mown right up to the edge. The sort of hedge you stop noticing, which is exactly the point.

Looking after it

The principle behind the considered clip is to do what the hedge needs and no more. A hedge that has lost its line often only needs a clean-up cut and a levelled top, not a drastic reduction. Cutting less, more often, keeps the growth even and the hedge healthy. In the Nailbourne valley, where hedges sit among chalk banks and old gardens, a restrained cut also keeps the hedge in character with its surroundings. We will always tell you if a lighter cut will do the job.
