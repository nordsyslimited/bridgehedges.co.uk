## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specificity

- Explained why Lonicera nitida is widespread in Bridge, Patrixbourne, Bekesbourne and Bishopsbourne gardens: planted as a box substitute after box blight and box tree moth took hold from mid-2000s onward.
- Covered the growth-rate split: valley-floor plots (deeper alluvial soil, more moisture) produce significantly more annual growth than chalk-slope gardens above Bridge, and need renovation more frequently.
- Included nesting law (Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, s.1, 1 March to 31 August) with practical timing guidance: ideal renovation windows are late August/September or January/February.
- Covered the staged two-season renovation approach for very neglected specimens.
- Included the Bifrons Park Conservation Area s.211 check (Town and Country Planning Act 1990) for very woody multi-stem growth.
- Referenced Bridge, Patrixbourne, Bekesbourne, Bishopsbourne, Lower Hardres, the Nailbourne, the old Watling Street and the North Downs chalk.
