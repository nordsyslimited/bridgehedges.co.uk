## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specificity notes

The article covers:
- Two-cut year: March/April for main red flush, optional June trim for second flush; avoid after mid-August.
- Nesting law: both spring and June cuts fall within the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 section 1 window (1 March to 31 August). Article covers the nest-check practice and how to handle the timing tension between spring red flush and nesting season.
- Leaf spot (Entomosporium mespili): more prevalent on humid valley-floor sites (lower Bridge, toward Bekesbourne) where morning mist persists. Chalk slopes above Bridge are less affected. Includes practical management: clean cuts, remove fallen infected leaves, a late-summer light trim to remove heavily infected growth.
- Growth rate differential: valley-floor photinia grows faster and produces more arisings per maintenance cut than chalk-slope equivalents.
- Hard renovation: older Bridge gardens often contain neglected inherited Red Robin screens. The article covers the case for a hard cut-back over two springs rather than gradual reduction, directly referencing the Garden Ninja technique.
- Conservation area: Bifrons Park CA check for multi-stem specimens exceeding 75mm stem at 1.5m.
