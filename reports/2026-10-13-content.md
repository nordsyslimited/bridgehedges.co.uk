## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specificity

- Covered the chalk slope vs valley floor species split: hawthorn, blackthorn, field maple, wayfaring tree, spindle and dog rose for chalk slopes; elder and dogwood for valley floor enrichment.
- Identified wayfaring tree (Viburnum lantana) and spindle (Euonymus europaeus) as chalk specialists genuinely native to the North Downs landscape around Bridge, unusual in nursery mixes but relevant to local hedgerow character.
- Named the wildlife value in the context of the Kent Downs AONB: winter thrushes (fieldfare, redwing) using hawthorn and blackthorn for shelter, bullfinch on sloe, warblers on elder berry crops.
- Covered timing for cell-grown planting around Bridge: September to November preferred on valley floor to avoid waterlogged January planting; chalk slopes more flexible.
- Explained the Nailbourne winterbourne's effect on valley-floor moisture and planting timing.
- Included nesting law (Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, s.1, 1 March to 31 August) applied to newly established hedges.
- Covered Bifrons Park Conservation Area: planting does not require notice; removal that preceded replanting may have done.
- Referenced Bridge, Patrixbourne, Bekesbourne, Bishopsbourne, Kingston, Barham, Lower Hardres, the Nailbourne, the North Downs, the Kent Downs AONB and the old Roman road through Bridge.
