## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specificity notes

The article covers:
- Timing: nesting law (Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 s.1, 1 March to 31 August), plus the added nuance that Ligustrum ovalifolium flowers June to July, attracting pollinators, so the main annual cut is best placed in late August to early September.
- Native wild privet (Ligustrum vulgare) context: genuinely native to chalk downland of southern England, including the North Downs. Drought-tolerant, suits the thin chalk soils above Bridge toward Lower Hardres and Patrixbourne.
- Chalk slope vs valley floor growth rates: garden privet grows more vigorously on the damper, deeper alluvial valley-floor soil of the Nailbourne valley than on well-drained chalk above, affecting cut frequency and arisings volume.
- Hard pruning: privet tolerates hard cuts in late winter before nesting season, unlike Leylandii. Comparable approach to laurel, cross-referenced.
- Bifrons Park Conservation Area: maintained privet hedges are below the section-211 threshold, but specimen multi-stem privets near or within the CA boundary that have exceeded 75mm stem diameter need six-week notice to Canterbury City Council.
- Toxicity of berries: practical note for gardens used by children or dogs.
