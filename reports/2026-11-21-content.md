## Video-to-topic mapping rationale

The "Pruning Variegated Plants" video by Burncoose Nurseries covers reversion removal on variegated shrubs: identifying plain-green reversions, tracing them to the base junction, removing them entirely. This maps directly to pittosporum tenuifolium because (1) the most commonly planted pittosporum tenuifolium cultivars in Kent gardens are variegated ('Silver Queen', 'Irene Paterson', 'Garnettii'), (2) reversion removal is the principal species-specific maintenance task for these cultivars beyond routine clipping, and (3) Burncoose sells a wide range of pittosporum and their channel is an authoritative UK source for the species.

## Bridge / Nailbourne-valley specificity

The article addresses:

1. **Reversion timing and monitoring** - reversion-removal pass in late spring, before shoots have produced side branches.
2. **Nesting-season timing conflict** - second cut in late August falls inside the nesting season (Wildlife & Countryside Act 1981, s.1, 1 March to 31 August). Article recommends pushing the cut into the first week of September.
3. **Chalk slope vs valley floor frost hardiness** - H4 rating adequate on free-draining chalk slopes above Bridge; valley-floor frost-pocket (Bishopsbourne, Barham, Kingston) increases risk. Cold-air drainage explained.
4. **Variegated forms marginally less hardy than green species** - noted with 'Tom Thumb' dark-purple form as more frost-tolerant alternative for exposed positions.
5. **Pittosporum as box substitute** - for gardens that have lost box to blight/box tree moth, with caveats on growth rate and drought tolerance.
6. **Bifrons Park Conservation Area** - s.211 Town and Country Planning Act 1990, six-week notice, Canterbury City Council. Maintenance trims rarely reach threshold; structural renovation on old specimens requires check.
