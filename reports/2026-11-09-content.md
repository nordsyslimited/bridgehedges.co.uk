### Topic rationale

Ceanothus (California lilac) is a common wall shrub in UK gardens and a frequent source of pruning queries. The timing rules are specific: evergreen types (the majority) must be pruned immediately after flowering in May or June, within the nesting season; deciduous types are pruned in early spring. The old-wood rule (ceanothus will not regenerate from old bare wood) makes getting the timing right genuinely consequential. No existing slug covered this plant.

Bridge-specific angle: chalk soil (the dominant soil type across the North Downs around Bridge) is well-suited to ceanothus, in contrast to acid-loving plants such as camellias. This makes the plant unusually common and successful in Bridge gardens, particularly on south-facing walls on the chalk slopes above the Nailbourne, which strengthens the local relevance. The nesting-season conflict for spring-flowering types is a natural crosslink to the nesting-law article.
