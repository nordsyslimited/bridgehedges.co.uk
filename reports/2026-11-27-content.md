### Why this topic

Viburnum opulus (guelder rose) is native to the chalk-stream valleys of SE England and grows wild in the Nailbourne valley hedgerows running through Bridge and Patrixbourne. It is distinct from Viburnum tinus (laurustinus), already covered in a separate article: V. opulus is deciduous, berry-producing, and subject to different pruning timing because of the wildlife value of its autumn berries. No existing slug covered this species.

### Bridge-specific angles developed

- Nailbourne valley as prime native V. opulus habitat (chalk winterbourne valley floor, damp but free-draining)
- Pruning-time conflict with WCA 1981 s.1 nesting season (flowers late spring, inside 1 March to 31 August window)
- Berry-wildlife trade-off: red berries are winter food for redwings, fieldfares, mistle thrushes; case for deferring autumn cuts on native plants
- Viburnum beetle (Pyrrhalta viburni) established in SE England: relevance to timing and spring growth recovery after rejuvenation
- s.211 TCPA 1990 for Bifrons Park CA specimens exceeding 75mm stem at 1.5m (less common on V. opulus but possible on old valley-floor plants)
- Native V. opulus vs ornamental 'Roseum' (sterile snowball bush): same pruning technique, different berry outcome
