## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specificity

- Nesting-season timing conflict: V. tinus flowers November to April; natural post-flowering cut in late April/May falls at the nesting-season opening (Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 s.1, 1 March to 31 August). Dense evergreen structure makes it a highly attractive nesting site for wrens, robins, blackbirds.
- 'Eve Price' as the dominant Kent hedge cultivar; compact, pink-budded, very hardy
- Valley-floor frost-pocket consideration: late March/April frost can brown opening buds on a valley-floor plant (cosmetic, not structural)
- 'Gwenllian' as an alternative for exposed valley-floor positions
- Bifrons Park Conservation Area s.211 TCA 1990: older estate specimens can exceed 75mm stem-diameter threshold on neglected or historic plantings
- Viburnum beetle (Pyrrhalta viburni): increasingly common SE England pest; management approach described; does not change pruning approach but affects summer appearance
- Chalk slope vs valley floor: waterlogging risk, moisture retention, summer stress differences noted
- Cross-reference to nesting-law guide and conservation-area guide
