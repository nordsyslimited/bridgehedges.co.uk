## Coverage gap filled

Thuja plicata (western red cedar) is a gap in existing coverage. The site has a Leylandii/conifer article from the same best4hedging "A focus on..." series but no Thuja-specific article. Thuja is a materially different species: slower growth rate (30-45cm/year vs 60-90cm for Leylandii), ability to regenerate from older wood (Leylandii cannot), and a contact-sensitisation risk specific to this species (plicatic acid / thujone). All three differences are practically significant for a Bridge garden owner.

## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specifics added

- Skin-sensitisation warning (plicatic acid / thujone): gloves, long sleeves, eye protection mandated
- Nesting-season timing: 1 March to 31 August, Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 s.1; September main cut advised
- Chalk slopes vs valley floor: Atrovirens tolerates chalk well; valley floor near Bishopsbourne/Bekesbourne grows faster and may need two cuts
- Old-wood regeneration advantage and its limits (very old brown-stemmed material may not respond)
- High Hedges: Anti-social Behaviour Act 2003, Part 8; Thuja falls within Act as evergreen
- Bifrons Park CA: s.211 TCPA 1990; 75mm stem-diameter threshold for notices
