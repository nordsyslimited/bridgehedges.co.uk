### Topic rationale

Bay laurel (Laurus nobilis) is an uncovered species in the existing how-to set. It is distinct from cherry laurel (Prunus laurocerasus) and Portugal laurel (Prunus lusitanica), both already covered. Bay laurel is used in formal SE England gardens as clipped hedges, standards and topiary, and is well suited to the chalk soils of the North Downs. The frost-pocket conditions on the Nailbourne valley floor create a genuinely local pruning-timing constraint not addressed by any general UK guide: late May is a safer start date than the typical "after the last frost" advice because the Nailbourne valley floor collects cold air draining off the North Downs slopes, producing later and harder frosts than the general guidance anticipates.

No existing slug covered bay laurel pruning. The two nearest existing articles (pruning-cherry-laurel-hedge and portugal-laurel-hedge-trimming) were edited to add a contextual sentence linking to the new article where the topic is a natural continuation.
