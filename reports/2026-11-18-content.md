## Bridge/Nailbourne-valley specificity

- Chalk-soil pH problem for H. macrophylla: alkaline North Downs chalk (pH 7.5+) causes slow growth, reluctant flowering, and colour shift from blue to muddy pink in blue/purple cultivars
- H. paniculata as the correct species choice for chalk gardens in Bridge: handles alkaline soil, more drought-tolerant than macrophylla, late-season flowering (August/September) fills a gap
- Specific cultivar recommendations for chalk: 'Limelight', 'Kyushu', 'Bobo', 'Phantom'
- Nesting-season timing conflict: paniculata's late-February/March pruning window opens right at the nesting season; check for nests before cutting, complete work before 1 March where possible
- Frost-pocket risk on Nailbourne valley floor: macrophylla breaks early and can catch late frosts in Bishopsbourne/Barham; paniculata is later into growth and genuinely hardy to below -20C
- 'Annabelle' (H. arborescens) note for valley-floor gardens: handles chalk moderately well but needs staking in exposed positions
- Bifrons Park Conservation Area note: pruning shrub-grown hydrangeas is not notifiable under s.211 TCA 1990 (applies only to trees above 75mm stem-diameter threshold); provides useful reassurance for conservation-area gardens
- Winter-interest note specific to Bridge: dried paniculata heads through October to February, valuable for gardens visible from the road in the conservation area
