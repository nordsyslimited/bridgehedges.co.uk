About

Hedges done considered. Locally.

Bridge Hedges & Tree Services is run by Richard Lim. Honest, no upselling, specified for the North Downs ground this village actually sits on.

What I do

I cut hedges. I reduce overgrown ones. And I plant new ones, with species chosen for the chalk and frost pockets of the Nailbourne valley, not whatever the nursery has in stock.

That is it. I do not do tree surgery beyond the pruning and reduction work described here, lawn care, fencing or the rest of the garden-services bundle. Hedges are a craft worth being good at, and I keep my focus narrow so I am.

Why local matters here

Bridge sits in the Nailbourne valley on the old Roman Watling Street, the London to Dover road, roughly two and a half miles south-east of Canterbury. Part of the village falls inside the Bifrons Park Conservation Area, and the section-211 rules apply to any tree over 75mm at chest height there. The North Downs chalk above the village is thin and sharply alkaline; the valley bottom by the Nailbourne holds more moisture and is a genuine frost pocket on a still winter night. Species that thrive on the open downland, yew, hawthorn, beech, can sulk or fail down by the stream, and the reverse is true too.

A contractor without that patch-specific knowledge will not know any of it without being told. I work this ground every week. The conservation-area paperwork is included. Nesting-law timing is built into how I schedule work through spring and summer. The species recommendations match what actually thrives on chalk and in a frost pocket, not a generic planting list.

How I work

  • Photos and postcode for an initial quote. I can give a fixed price for most routine cuts without coming out. Reductions usually need a site visit (free, no obligation).
  • Fixed-price quotes. No day-rate creep. If the job runs longer than I expected, that is my problem, not yours.
  • Conservation-area paperwork handled. If your address needs a section-211 notice to Canterbury City Council, I file it. Cost included in the quote.
  • Nesting-aware timing. A quick check before any March-August work, and honest advice on what should wait for autumn.
  • All arisings cleared. Clippings go to Canterbury's Vauxhall Road recycling centre or get chipped, never burned on site.
  • Bank transfer or cash. Invoice or receipt either way.

What you will not get

  • A salesman trying to upsell a "garden makeover."
  • A quote that doubles when I arrive.
  • A botched cut to compensate for not telling you the hedge actually needs a reduction.
  • A quiet job in June that ignores the nesting law.
